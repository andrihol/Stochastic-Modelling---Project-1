
# Stochastic Modelling – Project 1

**TMA4265 – Stochastic Modelling | NTNU | Fall 2026**

## Overview

Academic group project exploring stochastic
processes through mathematical modelling,
computer simulations, and statistical analysis.

The project focuses on two applications:
epidemic modelling using Markov chains and
insurance claim modelling using Poisson processes.

## Technologies

- Python
- R
- Jupyter Notebook
- NumPy, Matplotlib and Pandas
- Statistical simulation and analysis

## Project Components

### 1. Markov Chains and Epidemic Modelling

Developed and simulated stochastic models
describing the spread of an infectious disease
through a population.

The work included:
- Constructing transition probability matrices
- Analysing stationary distributions and
  long-run behaviour of Markov chains
- Simulating Susceptible-Infected-Recovered
  (SIR) population dynamics
- Estimating maximum infection levels and
  the time until peak infection
- Computing 95% confidence intervals
- Investigating the effects of different
  vaccination levels on disease spread

### 2. Poisson Processes and Insurance Claims

Applied Poisson processes to model the
frequency and monetary value of insurance claims.

The work included:
- Modelling claim arrivals using a homogeneous
  Poisson process
- Computing exact probabilities and comparing
  them with Monte Carlo simulations
- Simulating cumulative insurance claim amounts
  using a compound Poisson process
- Estimating the probability of total claims
  exceeding a specified threshold
- Analysing claims exceeding a given value
  using Poisson process thinning

## Project Structure

- `Python/` – Jupyter notebooks containing
  simulations, statistical analysis and plots
- `R/` – Implementations of Markov chain and
  epidemic simulations
- `Project_Report.pdf` – Mathematical
  derivations, results and visualizations

## Key Results

- Estimated long-run state distributions
  using Markov chain simulations
- Analysed epidemic dynamics and demonstrated
  how vaccination reduces peak infection levels
- Compared theoretical probabilities with
  simulated results for insurance claims
- Applied confidence intervals to quantify
  uncertainty in stochastic simulations
